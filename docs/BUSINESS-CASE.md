# CleanMargin business case
Research checked October 6, 2026. USD unless stated otherwise.

## Decision
Build a focused subscription tool for independent commercial cleaning operators with roughly 5–30 recurring locations. Start with office, education, warehouse, and community facilities. Sell a monthly contract review workflow: identify incomplete costs, measure contract contribution, investigate overruns, and prepare a scope/price discussion.

Launch price hypothesis: **$49/month**, one owner, up to 100 contracts and 1,200 saved monthly reviews. This is a hypothesis, not validated willingness to pay. Billing is not active in this build.

Confidence is reasonably high that this is a real software category and a recognizable problem. Confidence that this particular product will attract profitable customers is unproven. There is no basis to guarantee revenue or claim we can dominate competitors.

## What the research actually establishes
Established vendors sell cleaning-business software. Smaller competitors sell both software and spreadsheets for the same margin problem. This confirms visible supply and pricing, not total market size, actual customer counts, or demand for CleanMargin. We have not interviewed customers, independently audited competing products, purchased their plans, or measured CleanMargin conversion.

## Specific customer and purchase trigger
Buyer: an owner who personally reviews pricing, has several recurring contracts, and already has timesheets or monthly hour totals. Their accountant's business-wide profit-and-loss statement does not answer which location needs a scope review this month.

Trigger: wages or scope change; the owner suspects one site is absorbing too many hours; a contract renewal is approaching.

Exclude initially:
- Owners with one or two locations, no recurring review habit, or no usable cost records.
- Buyers primarily needing payroll, dispatch, GPS, accounting integration, or enterprise permissions.
- Healthcare compliance workflows and public procurement where a simple cost model is insufficient.
- Businesses outside the permitted-business policy.

## Why this direction under a $500 launch budget
These are strategic judgments, not measured market rankings:
- Consumer subscriptions generally require many more small payments and a distribution advantage we have not demonstrated.
- A marketplace introduces two-sided acquisition, supply quality, and fulfillment work.
- Broad AI wrappers are easy to compare and copy, and can create variable API costs.
- A focused B2B tool can be demonstrated directly against one business decision with founder-led sales. This reduces cash needed, but it increases the founder's time commitment.

## Proposed competitive advantage to test
1. First useful result from one location in under ten minutes.
2. Keep existing scheduling and payroll processes; import monthly totals through a documented CSV template.
3. Preserve the assumptions in each saved month rather than silently rewriting history.
4. Clearly distinguish missing costs, recorded results, and modeled pricing changes.
5. Turn a finding into a scope/price discussion without exposing internal wages or margins.

These are product goals, not proven superiority claims. CSV import is not a native integration. Competitors can reproduce these features.

A defensible advantage would have to develop through strong onboarding, specific bookkeeping/referral relationships, repeat usage, and customer trust. Source code and formulas alone provide little protection, particularly in a public repository.

## Offer and recurring value
Suggested pitch: "Review your cleaning contracts each month, see which fall below your margin target, and prepare a clear discussion before the next renewal."

Paid pilot: $49 for a clearly defined month, with disclosed renewal terms if subscription billing is enabled. Offer one onboarding session with the founder. Avoid a permanent labor-intensive managed service at software pricing.

Return each month for a completed cost review. If owners only use the calculator once, subscription retention is unlikely; consider a one-time product instead.

Never claim a modeled gap is recovered money. A proposed $400 increase may be rejected, delayed, offset by costs, or inappropriate under the contract.

## Unit economics: scenarios, not forecasts
Planning allowances: $4/month variable cost per paying account, including infrastructure and payment-processing allowance; separate fixed costs below. Neither allowance is a verified vendor quote. Support is valued at $30/hour and 20 minutes per customer per month. Founder acquisition, development, taxes, refunds, and other overhead are excluded.

| Paying accounts | Monthly revenue at $49 | Variable allowance | Fixed allowance | Before support | Support allowance | After modeled support |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 10 | $490 | $40 | $35 | $415 | $100 | $315 |
| 50 | $2,450 | $200 | $75 | $2,175 | $500 | $1,675 |
| 200 | $9,800 | $800 | $250 | $8,750 | $2,000 | $6,750 |

At $35 contribution/account after modeled support, a $100 fully loaded acquisition cost takes approximately 2.86 months to repay, before fixed costs. If support doubles, the economics deteriorate. Cash CAC and founder-time CAC must be tracked separately. Small early samples cannot support reliable lifetime-value claims.

## Cash launch budget ceiling
- Domain and basic business email: reserve up to $60.
- Hosting/payment/testing contingency: reserve up to $100.
- Customer interview or onboarding contingency: reserve up to $90.
- Uncommitted reserve: $250.
- Paid ads: $0 until pilot evidence exists.

These are spending caps, not purchases or current price quotes. No spending or outreach has been performed.

## First 30 days: evidence before expansion
Days 1–5: identify 30 qualified operators through lawful public business directories or existing relationships. Conduct 10 short conversations. Ask them to show their last monthly contract review, not whether they "like the idea." Record current method, consequences of errors, data availability, decision authority, and what they pay today.

Days 6–10: demonstrate one real location with permission. Measure time to first complete review. Show the competitor alternatives openly. Ask for payment for a specific pilot month.

Days 11–20: onboard up to five pilots, watching each import. Fix repeated failures. Record actual support minutes. Do not promise automatic integrations that are absent.

Days 21–30: ask customers to complete another review, compare measured use, and ask whether they will continue paying. Collect permitted feedback; use no invented testimonials.

No messages should be sent automatically. Outreach text in LAUNCH.md is a draft for the founder to review and send.

## Gates
Proceed to broader sales only after:
- At least 3 unrelated businesses pay $49 for the pilot after seeing alternatives.
- At least 4 of the first 5 pilots complete a real monthly review.
- At least 3 of the first 5 choose to continue into a second paid period.
- Median assisted first-review time is under 15 minutes; target under 10 without assistance.
- Continuing support averages under 20 minutes/account/month.
- No unresolved data-isolation or incorrect-calculation issues.

Do not expand paid acquisition if fewer than 3 customers pay after 20 qualified demos. First examine audience, offer, workflow, and price. If the dominant objection is "my spreadsheet already does this," interview about a more valuable unresolved task or sell a one-time template. Do not hide weak evidence by adding features.

## Main failure modes
The $29 spreadsheet may be sufficient; import work may exceed perceived benefit; operators may not keep accurate costs; low margins may reflect operational problems that software cannot fix; customers may cancel once they reprice; the founder may dislike direct sales; established vendors may close the workflow gap.

## Launch boundaries
This build is a private pilot with saved workspaces and sample data. Before charging external customers: activate a payment provider and verify payment/cancellation flows, confirm supported customer sign-in, publish accurate service/privacy terms with the operator's identity and contact, verify backup/restore and retention, and perform an independent security review. Religious constraints are a product policy, not a certification.
