# Founder launch playbook

## The job for tomorrow
Find five commercial cleaning operators with 5–30 recurring locations. Ask for 15 minutes to understand their current cost-review process. Do not buy ads.

## Discovery questions
1. When did you last compare paid hours and costs against the price for each location?
2. Show me the last spreadsheet or report you used.
3. What happened the last time a contract took more hours than expected?
4. Who supplies the monthly hours and costs, and in what format?
5. What would make your current method worth replacing?
6. After the demo: would you pay $49 for one month using your own contracts?

## Outreach draft — not sent
Subject: How do you review margins by cleaning location?

Hi [name],

I'm building CleanMargin for small commercial cleaning operators. It compares the monthly price for a location with actual hours, supplies, and overhead, then helps prepare a scope or pricing review.

I'm looking for feedback from owners who already track costs. Could you show me how you review one contract today? A 15-minute conversation would help me see whether the workflow is useful.

If it fits, the proposed pilot is $49 for one month. There is no promise of savings or new contracts.

[Your name and real business contact]

Use truthful identity, lawful contact practices, and any required opt-out. Do not mass-send this draft or imply an existing relationship.

## Demo, 8 minutes
- Ask the owner to choose a completed month and one recurring location.
- Enter their agreed price, planned paid hours, wage, payroll burden, supplies, overhead, and target margin.
- Enter actual completed-month costs.
- Explain which expenses are included and which are absent.
- Compare the result with their existing spreadsheet.
- Explore a scope or price scenario without automatically changing the agreement.
- Download the client discussion draft.
- Ask what would stop them from doing this next month.

## Pilot log
Track prospect source, qualification, current alternative, interview date, demo date, paid amount, onboarding minutes, first completed review, second review, renewal decision, and cancellation reason.

Keep real customer details out of this public repository. Store the log in the founder's private system.

## Close without pressure
"If this saves you enough work to use each month, the pilot is $49 for the month. If your spreadsheet already does the job, that's useful feedback too."

## Customer acquisition beyond the first five
Test one narrow channel at a time: cleaning-industry bookkeepers, local industry networks, or permission-based referrals. Do not claim a partnership before it exists. A free calculator can support discovery, but conversion from free tools to subscriptions is unproven.

## Before accepting a payment
Configure the actual provider account, exact plan price, billing interval, refund policy, and cancellation method. Test a successful payment, failed payment, renewal, cancellation, and access after cancellation. Provide real operator contact details. No live payment system is currently connected.
